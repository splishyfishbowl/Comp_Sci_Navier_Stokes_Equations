\begin{center}
    \textbf{Abstract}
    
\end{center}
    The simulation of fluid dynamics is a classical physical problem and is described by the Navier-Stokes equations. 
    Nevertheless, it is still a matter of research as it is hard to solve.
    Analytical solutions exist only for specific simple cases.
    In more general cases, numerical methods are necessary to solve the system of partial differential equations.
    Here, not only the convergence of the method but also the computational efficiency are crucial to model accurate physical behavior.
    In this work, we use Operator splitting and Spectral methods to obtain numerical solutions of two-dimensional incompressible fluid dynamics.
    First, we deal with linear advection and implement Lie-Splitting to gain computational efficiency.
    Further, we then extend this approach to respect the incompressibility constraint by using Chorin's projection method.
    Here we obtain a Poisson equation that enables us to correct the pressure field to match the incompressible characteristics.
    In particular, the solution of such Poisson equation is performed in two different ways which we evaluate.
    We (1) use the Jacobi method and find that it is not suitable for our desired degree of accuracy. 
    We therefore (2) implement spectral methods to address this issue.
    Finally, these improvements let us reproduce Rayleigh-B\'{e}nard Convection cells and ensure that the incompressibility constraint is satisfied.
